WEIGHTED   AVERAGE   BASE  WAGE
Applied  To   Home   Care   Services
By
Michael J. Lubrano, Jr., P.D.



This analysis describes how a weighted averaging program was developed for Suffolk County care at home services. Home care case coverage shortages persisted even though contract agencies were given periodic increases in their rate for service. A system was developed that was able to ensure and monitor that agency rate increases were translated into worker salary increases and resulted in a significant positive impact on case coverage. The program was effective for recruiting and retaining workers and similar programs can be developed for other types of contracted services that require a system to monitor how contract agency rates or fees relate to worker salaries and services provided.

INTRODUCTION



Care At Home services can be a cost effective alternative to nursing home care as well as a means for a preferable quality of life. Although one-on-one service by a Home Care Worker, e.g. a Personal Care Aide (PCA) or Home Health Aide (HHA), can become more expensive on a 24/7 basis, many cases have family or other support systems and need limited services that make Home Care a preferred alternative financially as well as socially. 1

CHAPTER I

OBJECTIVES AND GOALS

Program Objectives and Goals

Program objectives include the development and refinement of Cost Effective, Cost Efficient and Cost Containment alternatives for medically needed services. In Suffolk County, both social and health aspects of the New York State Program of Medical Assistance for Needy Persons, more commonly called the Medical Assistance (Medicaid) Program, were joined in one Medical Assistance Administration (MAA), later renamed the Medicaid Services Division (MSD). 1   This MAA or MSD was charged with the responsibility to interpret and implement the laws, regulations and policies governing medical services provided within the scope of the program.   The MAA or MSD is therefore responsible for seeing that appropriate high quality health care is available to Medicaid eligible persons at the lowest possible cost and with a minimum of administrative delay. 2


Medical Assistance Goals

The Quality Utilization Review (QUR) section of MAA is specifically responsible for reviewing the quality of medical care and monitoring reimbursements. It must also take full advantage of cost containment procedures to help alleviate the financial burden of these mandated services.

A backlog in home care coverage often translates into a backlog and/or delay in nursing facility care discharges which in turn results in a backlog and/or delay in hospital discharges. There is a constant need for increased home care case coverage to replace more costly alternatives that are being provided simply because there is a shortage of home care workers. The goal was to develop a system and contract language to improve program coverage as well as to ensure and monitor contract compliance. 3

CHAPTER II

BACKGROUND INFORMATION

Legal Basis and Enabling Legislation

The Suffolk County Department of Social Services (DSS) was created under the County Charter and the New York State Social Services Law. The Department must function within the guidelines of the Federal Social Security Act of 1935 and its amendments. It also comes under the jurisdiction of the United States Department of Health and Human Services, and the New York State Department of Social Services. The Suffolk County DSS home care program authorizes and monitors Personal Care Services. As of May 1992 services were being provided to approximately 2,000 Medicaid eligible elderly and disabled clients annually. These people have impairments requiring that they receive assistance with activities of daily living in order for them to be maintained safely in the home. Services must be ordered by a physician, performed by a trained Personal Care Worker in accordance with a DSS approved plan of care, and supervised by a registered professional nurse. Personal Care services are provided pursuant to Title XIX of the United States Social Security Act Article 5, Title II of the New York State Social Services Law, 42 CFR 430.0 et seq. and 18 NYCRR 505.14. Personal Care Aide services are provided by agencies under contract to the Department of Social Services. 1

Automated Program

The conversion of the Public Assistance program to an electronic data processing (EDP) system led the way for the Medical Assistance Administration to implement a computerized system in 1976. Automation offered the potential for solving the problems associated with cost containment, the increasing claims volume, the availability of program information, and the development of a Management Information System (MIS). By 1981 the system had continued to improve and finally made it possible to link multiple databases containing information concerning client eligibility, claims payment and service history. As the automated program was being expanded, MAA made programmatic advances to incorporate other alternatives, such as using private contracts, controlling over-utilization, investigating fraud and abuse, developing a shared aide program, and requiring weighted average base wages in addition to minimum wages.

The State of Affairs

The Need for Services

The need for home care services is defined by both the number of cases i.e. individuals needing service and the total number of hours of care. There is a constant need for increased case coverage due to the increasing eligible population and the need for cost effective alternative levels of care. Home Care work is an entry-level job that generally starts at minimum wage yet requires training certification and background checks. Home Care Agencies are paid an hourly fee for service rate and the agency in turn pays the home care worker an hourly wage.

Increasing the number of contract agencies did not substantially increase case coverage. If an existing private agency signed with MAA the agency aides were already serving private pay cases that had private pay rates above the Medicaid rate. If a new agency signed with MAA, the majority of their aides would just be aides they enticed from other agencies so that there was little or no increase in coverage.

Increasing the rate paid to agencies did not generate increased case coverage either. If one agency raises its entry salary in order to attract new aides, that agency is more likely to attract aides working at another agency, than it is to attract new workers into the industry. If workers move from one agency to another there is no new net increase in coverage. Additionally, when the rate is increased, agencies that do not pass any or all of the increase to their workers, amass a windfall increase in profit per hour of service. There is no incentive for an agency to raise hourly wages unless or until their lower salary levels actually results in a decrease in their ability to cover the same number of hours covered when the rate was increased. 2

Organizational Behavior


Public and Private organizational behavior rarely follows rational acting which would optimize specific, ordered, central, unitary, rational, comprehensive, and/or value maximizing decisions. Programs, e.g. home care, should be planned and organized to get everybody pulling in the same direction, as much as possible, in order to improve efficiency and increase uniformity of available services. Budgets, taxes, laws, etc. fix the availability of financial and other resources at any given time. Cooperation and coordination between parties is not an automatic process. In practice, inherent competition and friction between organizations, departments or in this case contract agencies is bound to develop. 3

Home care agencies and workers try to cover as many hours as possible. However, there are many variables to coverage e.g. are the hours too early in the morning or too late at night, who covers weekends and holidays, how sick & how much personal care is needed on the specific case, the personality and cooperation of the client and the client s support, is the client male or female and is their a preference for a male or female worker, etc.

Any system devised would therefore have to include a means to level the playing field between contract agencies so that program and contract compliance would be uniform and capable of being monitored.

In an effort to recruit and bring workers into the industry, the MAA contract increased the agency rate and also began setting a minimum wage to be paid to PCA workers that was above NYS minimum wage. Although raising the minimum salary helped bring in new aides, there still was no incentive for aides to stay in the industry if higher paying jobs became available and competition left little room for the agencies to reward longevity or exceptional performance.




CHAPTER III

DEVELOPMENT OF THE WABW

New Minimums Set


The Hourly Minimum Wage in New York State was increased from $3.10 to $3.35 effective January 1, 1981 and was not increased again until it was increased from $3.35 to $3.80 effective April 1, 1990. 1    Effective July 1, 1988, the hourly rate for the Suffolk County Home Care 26 contract agencies, was increased to $11.10 per hour. In addition to the rate increase for agencies, new contract language was added that established a minimum wage and a weighted average base wage (WABW) to be paid to aides serving Suffolk County Medicaid cases. The minimum wage was established at $5.75 per hour. The WABW was established at 6.50 per hour. By setting these new standards, employers were provided flexibility to establish varying salaries and create a career ladder for their aides.

Reports Required


The 26 contract agencies were required to submit reports indicating both the total number of PCA hours worked on Suffolk Medicaid cases and the total number of dollars paid to the PCA s during the period from 7/1/88 to 6/30/89.

When mandated in each new contract, agencies would increase the minimum-starting wage being offered. In addition, there would always be a few PCA workers that were paid above the minimum wage mandated for longevity, exceptional performance or other positive reinforcement reasons. Whenever a contract agency was asked what was the average wage they were paying their aides, the contract agencies would just take the highest salary plus the lowest and divide by two. That result determines the straight average or arithmetic mean. Unfortunately, that number does not account for the difference in the number of aides at each salary level and would be almost meaningless programmatically since only a relatively few workers were paid at higher than the minimum wage salary. Furthermore, multiplying each salary by the number of aides paid that salary and then dividing by the total number of aides would result in the average salary weighted by the number of aides. That too would be almost meaningless programmatically since it could not be used to determine the actual amount of the agency hourly rate being passed to their aides.



CHAPTER IV

CONCLUSION

The Arithmetic Solution


The arithmetic solution was therefore to calculate the average salary weighted by the number of hours of service. This established a method to monitor the dollars per hour paid to workers in their wage in relation to the dollars per hour paid to the contract agency in their rate. Contract agencies were already able to track this information based on pre-existing internal financial records. In addition, once time was allowed for claims submission, as well as, payment and any post payment adjustments, MAA was able to verify this information from the MIS EDP reporting system.
